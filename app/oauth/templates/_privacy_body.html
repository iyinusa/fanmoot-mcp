What {{ public_base_url }} keeps when you connect an app or an assistant to your Fanmoot account, and how to take it back. Last updated {{ updated }}.

This service connects apps and assistants to Fanmoot, such as Alexa, Claude, ChatGPT and Cursor. It is not where your Fanmoot account lives. Your profile, your posts and your settings are held by fanmoot.com and covered by its own privacy notice. This page is about the service in between.

If you never connect an account

Scores, fixtures and match conversations need no sign-in. For each question an app asks, this service records which tool answered, how long it took, whether it worked and when. It does not record what was asked or what was answered. Your address is held in memory for up to an hour to enforce rate limits, and is never written down.

When you sign in or sign up

  • Your email address and password are passed to fanmoot.com once, to sign you in, and are never stored here.
  • A new account, if you create one here, is created by fanmoot.com. Nothing you type on the sign-up form is kept by this service.
  • A sign-in cookie on this site only, lasting {{ session_minutes }} minutes, so you can finish connecting without signing in twice. It is not used for anything else.

What is kept while an app is connected

  • Your Fanmoot user id and handle, to show you your connected apps. Not your email address or your name.
  • A Fanmoot session token, encrypted, so the app can act for you without asking for your password again. It is only ever decrypted to make a request you asked for.
  • The permissions you ticked for each app, and the app's own tokens. The tokens are stored as one-way hashes, so they cannot be read back even here. An app's access token lasts {{ access_hours }} hour{{ "s" if access_hours != 1 }} and its refresh token {{ refresh_days }} days.
  • A record of each decision you make about an app, such as allowing, declining, adding a permission or disconnecting. Each one is kept with the time, your address and your browser's name, because "did I really allow this?" is a question worth being able to answer.
  • The same tool record as above, marked with which connection made the call. Still no content.

What you post

A take, reply, reaction, prediction or follow is sent to fanmoot.com as you, and fanmoot.com keeps it. This service does not keep a copy. To stop a double post it keeps a one-way fingerprint of the request for as long as fanmoot.com needs it, and that fingerprint cannot be turned back into your words.

Match mood

To say how a match thread feels, this service samples its public takes{% if mood_uses_bedrock %} and sends their text to Amazon Bedrock to be labelled positive, negative or neutral{% else %} and labels them with a word list{% endif %}. Only the words are used. No handle, name or id goes with them, subscriber-only takes are never included, and the labels are counted and then discarded.

How long things are kept

  • Tool records: {{ tool_call_days }} days.
  • Records of your decisions about apps: {{ consent_days }} days.
  • Your encrypted Fanmoot session token: until no app is connected with it or fanmoot.com ends the session. Then it is deleted and fanmoot.com is told to end it. That happens within a day of the last app being disconnected, and within a day of a sign-in that did not end in connecting anything.
  • An app that registered itself and was never connected to anyone: {{ dcr_days }} days.

Disconnecting

Go to connected apps to see every app linked to your account and disconnect any of them. The app's tokens stop working at once. When no connected app is left using your Fanmoot session, the session token is deleted and fanmoot.com is told to end it, as above. Signing out of fanmoot.com everywhere also stops every app connected here from acting as you. To delete your Fanmoot account itself, use fanmoot.com.

Who else sees it

Nobody is sold anything. Your data goes to fanmoot.com, which is the point of the service. Public take text goes to Amazon Bedrock as described above. The servers this runs on keep ordinary network logs. An app you connect sees what its permissions allow and nothing else. Each app has its own privacy policy, and Fanmoot has not vetted the apps that register themselves.
